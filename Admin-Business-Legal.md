entrepreneurs and job-seekers, (6) connect students to career
mentors/employers early.

pitch/slide deck covering target market, financial model, business
fundamentals. Workforce Development — resume, portfolio, elevator pitch,
work shared with multiple employers. Entrepreneurship — the ability to
pitch, an idea with strong product-market fit.

## 6a. In-kind candidate analysis

Which budget line items could realistically be treated as in-kind
contributions rather than cash.

**Already effectively in-kind (VSU-provided):**
- TL Draw, GitHub, Arduino IDE (free tools).
- MS Office, Zoom (VSU-provided).
- Laptops (VSU-supplied directly, outside this budget).
- Classroom/lab space (a VSU responsibility, not a line item at all).

**Strong candidates to actively pursue as in-kind:**
- **Dr. Nicholson's compensation (TBD)** — if this falls under his
  existing VSU role, it may not need a new cash line at all.
- **Insurance ($4,000)** — VSU likely already carries institutional
  liability/campus insurance; worth checking whether it extends to
  cover this program's activities.
- **Legal Fees ($4,000)** — VSU's own counsel needs to review the
  Agreement on VSU's side regardless; presumably already in-kind.
- **Event venue costs** (part of the $10,000 Events line) — if events
  happen on VSU's campus, the space is likely already covered; the
  $10,000 may really only need to cover catering/materials.
- **Photography/Videography ($4,999)** — many universities have
  in-house communications/media staff who could cover this.
- **Print Materials ($500)** — possible VSU print-shop in-kind.
- **Hardware/sensor kits (~$4,400)** — STEM hardware vendors (Adafruit,
  SparkFun, etc.) sometimes donate kits for educational programs — a
  real possibility, not yet arranged.
- **Part of the Marketing budget** — VSU is already expected to lead
  pre-enrollment/enrollment marketing to its own student body; only
  Program-specific marketing (social media, program page) needs new
  cash.

**Should stay cash:**
- Instructional compensation (Javon/Andrew/Emanuel) — real personal
  income, not a donation candidate.
- Employee Travel, Checkr/SentryLink background checks, the Visa Gift
  Card program (direct student benefit), ChatGPT Plus/Lovable Business
  subscriptions (unless a specific vendor sponsorship gets negotiated).

## 8. External funding sources

**Context**: VSU is an HBCU and cannot absorb the full program cost
(~$295K-$311K) alone, and isn't looking to push the Program's budget
down further — the path is external funding and in-kind contributions,
not cost-cutting.

**Concrete leads:**
- **Dominion Energy** — has expressed interest in helping fund a
  program like this. Needs formal follow-up.
- **Siemens** — direct outreach in progress.
- **Virginia Gateway Region (VGR)** — the regional economic development
  organization for the Tri-Cities area; strong natural fit given the
  program's regional focus, and currently well-funded.
- **"Trilus"** — name as given; spelling/exact organization needs
  confirming before outreach.
- **Virginia Credit Union** — Richmond-based, plausible community/CSR
  giving program.
- **Cameron Foundation** (Petersburg, VA) — strong fit: service area is
  literally the Tri-Cities footprint this program is built around,
  funds education/economic development, has funded STEM education
  before.
- **NSF HBCU-UP** (federal) — funds STEM undergraduate education
  specifically at HBCUs, including "Targeted Infusion Projects" for
  short-term, well-defined goals.
- **Southern Company** — already has a relationship with VSU (funded a
  $500,000 endowed professorship in sustainability) — a warm lead.
- **American Electric Power Foundation** and **Dudley T. Dougherty
  Foundation** — both fund STEM education but geographic fit
  (Tri-Cities/Petersburg specifically) isn't yet confirmed.
- **Anthropic's Claude for Education / Claude Corps** — in-kind
  (credits/licenses), not cash, but reduces the software-subscription
  gap by the same amount. Claude Corps gives host organizations a
  $10,000 grant + $2,500/fellow in credits, plus an Anthropic-paid
  fellow for a year; the January 2027 cohort aligns with this program's
  start. **These programs require an institutional applicant (VSU),
  not a private LLC partner.**

Also worth asking whether VSU's own institutional advancement/
development office has other regional corporate or foundation contacts
— the Tri-Cities employers already listed in §4 (Amazon, LEGO Group,
Perdue Farms, etc.) are plausible CSR/education-funding targets too.

**Note on sequencing**: stakeholder outreach is proceeding before formal
sign-off from VSU's own advancement/fundraising office. Most funders
(foundations especially) typically expect VSU itself to be the
applicant/recipient of record, and a public university's advancement
office usually wants to coordinate/approve external asks made in its
name. Worth framing early conversations as exploratory/preliminary
rather than firm commitments on VSU's behalf until that office is
looped in.
