# Regional Economic Development Stakeholder Deck — Outline

**Tri-Cities Innovation Lab (VSU Innovation Program)**

**Audience:** Virginia Gateway Region, School of Business, School of Agriculture, Open Trellis, Cameron Foundation, and related regional-development stakeholders.

## Core Narrative

Students gain technical skills (IoT, AI) and business skills, applied directly to key industries in the Tri-Cities and Southside region. The deck should leave stakeholders believing that investing in this program means access to vetted, region-ready talent and visible growth of a key regional economy — without overstating the program as an established "economic anchor" before it has track record, and without stepping on VSU's academic territory.

Structural logic for the middle of the deck follows a **Why → How → What** sequence: the Problem/Opportunity slide is the Why, How Do We Keep Talent in the Region is the How, and What Is the Program is the What.

## 1. Title Slide

**Purpose:** Orient the room before anything else starts.

- Program name, prominently featured
- Partner logos (Builder Tech, Voltpop, Explay, VSU)
- Date
- Neutral descriptor (e.g. "Stakeholder Briefing") — should read as informational, not a sales pitch

## 2. Overview

**Purpose:** Set expectations for how the conversation will flow and what decision the room will be asked to make.

- Preview the deck's flow: Why → How → What, then Target Students, Timeline, and the Ask
- Frame the meeting as leading toward a specific decision, not just a presentation

## 4. Problem / Opportunity ("Why")

**Purpose:** Create urgency with data that's difficult to challenge — framed as opportunity rather than problem, and without insulting VSU. This slide stays strictly on the issue; no industries or solutions are named yet (those come later).

- **Core story:** talent is trained in the region and then leaves — a regional brain-drain narrative
- **Infographic:** students entering VSU (or entering school generally) and then exiting the region/industry — shows outflow only; the inflow / "staying" visual is reserved for the How slide
- Framed to create urgency without directly criticizing VSU (e.g. avoid emphasizing limited collaboration between VSU's business and engineering departments as a talking point for this audience)

**Data points needed, with feasibility:**
- % of VSU engineering/business graduates who leave the region after graduation — Medium feasibility (VSU career services / institutional research may have partial data)
- % of graduates without a job lined up at graduation (underemployment) — Medium feasibility
- Employer-reported technical skills gap (IoT/AI) in the region — Higher feasibility (VGR / GO Virginia labor market data)
- Existing pipeline gaps between VSU coursework and regional employer needs — Low feasibility as a hard statistic; likely narrative only

## 5. How Do We Keep Talent in the Region ("How")

**Purpose:** Show the mechanism — what the program actually does to retain talent regionally. This is the payoff/solution slide.

- **Infographic:** students staying in the region / entering the regional workforce — the counterpart to the "leaving" visual on the Problem slide
- **Five key regional industries targeted:** Agriculture, Manufacturing, Logistics, Energy & Utilities, Healthcare Technology — tied together by the IoT/AI thread running through the program
- **Note:** skills taught also transfer broadly beyond these five industries — general business/project management principles, pitching and presentations, entrepreneurship skills, data analysis and visualization, and applied AI
- **Mechanism:** students complete project-based work aligned to real employer needs; employers participate as judges/evaluators; students pitch, present, and share resumes directly to companies and recruiters — so students become known quantities to these employers, not strangers
- **Soft skills** developed alongside technical skills: project management, communication, storytelling
- **Framing:** Competency, Compatibility, and Commitment — commitment is achieved because students choose their own projects, tackling problems they genuinely care about (placement of this framing may shift once content is drafted)

**Data point needed, with feasibility:**
- National demand for IoT/AI-related talent — High feasibility
- Virginia state-level demand data — Medium-to-high feasibility (Virginia Employment Commission, GO Virginia)
- Tri-Cities / Southside-specific demand data — Low feasibility; likely not available at this granularity

## 6. What Is the Program ("What")

**Purpose:** Explain what the program actually is and does.

- What students learn and do: technical skills (IoT, AI) plus business skills, applied through project-based work
- What students walk away with
- Program duration
- Career-facing piece: the Tri-Cities Regional Day concept — employers come to VSU to participate in the pitch competition

## 7. Target Student Profile

**Purpose:** Clarify who the program develops and why — kept as its own slide rather than folded into the timeline.

- **Primary focus:** freshmen and sophomores — gives both students and employers more runway to develop over time and across multiple cohorts
- Juniors and seniors remain eligible to participate, but are not the primary focus
- **Priority schools/colleges:** Business, Engineering, and Agriculture — program is open to all VSU students, but recruitment prioritizes these three
- Employer feedback from each cohort feeds into refining the next, so hiring interest can build progressively across cohorts rather than being a one-shot evaluation

## 9. The Ask

**Purpose:** Present the specific ask. Base slide stays consistent; stakeholder-specific variants swap in depending on the audience (e.g. Dominion/Siemens vs. VGR/Cameron Foundation/Open Trellis).

**Note:** grant funding is being pursued separately — this ask is positioned as sponsorship in addition to, not instead of, that path.

- Financial contribution as a sponsor
- **In-kind:** mentorship time throughout the program
- **In-kind:** participation as judges and recruiters for the final pitch and career day (evaluating the pitch competition and giving students direct resume/career feedback)
- **In-kind:** equipment donation
- **In-kind:** direct cost coverage — stakeholder reviews the shared budget and chooses a specific line item to fund directly

Light callback to the Who We Are slide here — inviting those individuals to actually show up as part of the ask, without repeating their full bios.
