# 4D Use-Case Exercise

*Source: [`../Day1.md`](../Day1.md) item 5. ~25 min. First real
classroom exercise of the 4D framework anywhere in this course — if the
room hasn't seen 4D before, open with [Week 1's Prompting
Frameworks](../../Week1/Presentations/Prompting_Frameworks.md)'s 4D
section first. This deck applies it live; it doesn't re-teach it.*

---

## The question

- *Is this actually a good use case?*
- Not a mechanical "turn this into a paragraph" task — a judgment call.
  That's what 4D is for, not RTFC.

---

## Delegate

- Hand the **brainstorm** to Kiro.
- Keep the **final pick** as your own call.

---

## Describe

- Give Kiro real context, not a generic ask.
- *"Brainstorm 5 IoT use-case ideas for [a regional problem your team
  names], each with a rough sense/act loop."*
- Not: *"give me IoT ideas."*

> Speaker notes: same lesson as Week 1's bad-prompt demo — vague in,
> generic out — now applied to a judgment task instead of a mechanical
> one.

---

## Discern

- As a team, throw out the weak/generic ideas.
- Keep what's actually specific and locally grounded.

---

## Diligence

- Sanity-check what's left: can an ESP32 + a sensor + an app actually
  approximate this?
- In the ~13 weeks remaining, by a team with no EE/CS background?
- **If not, it's not a good pick yet** — back to Describe with tighter
  context.

---

## Carry this to Day 2

- Narrow to your **1–2 candidate use cases**.
- You'll map one of them and write it up on Day 2.
