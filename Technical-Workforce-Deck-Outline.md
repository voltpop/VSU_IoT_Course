## 5. How Do We Keep Talent in the Region

**Status:** Unchanged from Regional Economic Development deck.

**Purpose:** Show the mechanism — what the program actually does to retain talent regionally. This is the payoff/solution slide.

- **Infographic:** students staying in the region / entering the regional workforce — the counterpart to the "leaving" visual on the Problem slide
- **Five key regional industries targeted:** Agriculture, Manufacturing, Logistics, Energy & Utilities, Healthcare Technology — tied together by the IoT/AI thread running through the program
- **Note:** skills taught also transfer broadly beyond these five industries — general business/project management principles, pitching and presentations, entrepreneurship skills, data analysis and visualization, and applied AI
- **Mechanism:** students complete project-based work aligned to real employer needs; employers participate as judges/evaluators; students pitch, present, and share resumes directly to companies and recruiters — so students become known quantities to these employers, not strangers
- **Soft skills** developed alongside technical skills: project management, communication, storytelling
- **Framing:** Competency, Compatibility, and Commitment — commitment is achieved because students choose their own projects, tackling problems they genuinely care about (placement of this framing may shift once content is drafted)

**Data point needed, with feasibility:**
- National demand for IoT/AI-related talent — High feasibility
- Virginia state-level demand data — Medium-to-high feasibility (Virginia Employment Commission, GO Virginia)
- Tri-Cities / Southside-specific demand data — Low feasibility; likely not available at this granularity

## 6. Workforce Pipeline (NEW)

**Status:** New slide, added after How Do We Keep Talent in the Region. Also a candidate to add into the Regional deck. Title is a placeholder pending a final name.

**Purpose:** Show employers that they get direct visibility into real student thinking and skills, not certifications, so they can identify strong talent early and build multi-year relationships toward internships and hires.

- Students will not walk away with certifications — the program does not overpromise on that front
- What employers see instead: students' experience using the tools, and their ability to think across business and engineering contexts
- This cross-context fluency is treated as a soft skill: the ability to move between business and engineering thinking, and to tell a story about the work
- Employers get to observe this directly by attending career day and the pitch competition (the same event, referred to as "pitching career day")
- At the event, employers can see which teams are strongest, and review students' portfolios and resumes
- This gives employers visibility into how students think, articulate, and problem-solve — not just a blanket list of candidates

**On the engineering side:** employers can identify which engineering-track students are also able to grasp business concepts and lead teams and manage projects

**On the business side:** employers can identify which business-track students are able to articulate and incorporate a technical lens into their work

**For the School of Agriculture:** same dynamic applies, focused on the business and tech dimensions of agriculture (the program does not teach agricultural science itself, though some participating students will bring that background)

- Because the program's primary focus is freshmen, employers can look forward to watching students develop over time rather than needing them to be "ready" immediately — relationships can build across all four years, including internships
- The program will continuously mentor students and improve year over year to help them get hired, but this requires sponsoring companies' backing and their input on curriculum, so the program can align students to what employers actually need
- Ties back to the program's core framing: Competency, Compatibility, and Commitment — commitment shown through students choosing their own projects
