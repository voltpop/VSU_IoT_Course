# VSU Innovation Program — Stakeholder Notes

**This is research/contact prep, not a pitch deck.** For each
stakeholder: what they want or care about, how to reach them, current
outreach status, and (once known) a tailored framing angle. The actual
tailored presentation gets built later, from these notes, once enough
is known per stakeholder — not guessed at generically ahead of time.
Full funding-source reasoning and the underlying budget figures live in
`Admin-Business-Legal.md` §8/§6.

## Framing — read before any conversation

Stakeholder outreach is happening **before** VSU's own institutional
advancement/fundraising office has formally signed off. Foundations and
corporate funders typically expect the university itself to be the
applicant/recipient of record, and a public university's advancement
office usually wants to coordinate/approve external asks made in its
name.

- Frame every conversation right now as **exploratory/preliminary** —
  "we're building a program with VSU and exploring what partnership
  could look like" — not a firm ask made on VSU's behalf.
- Don't quote the total program cost (currently ~$295K–$311K, and still
  has open line items — see `Admin-Business-Legal.md` §6/§9) as a fixed,
  approved ask. Treat it as a working estimate.
- Be ready to say plainly that anything formal routes through VSU's own
  channels once its advancement office is looped in.
- Dr. Shawn Nicholson is VSU's actual Institutional Liaison — his
  presence/blessing on a call lends real legitimacy; worth considering
  for conversations beyond the earliest exploratory ones.
- **Hierarchy of what a stakeholder can offer (per VoltPop)** — if asked
  "what do you actually need," listen/ask in this order of usefulness,
  but don't lock in a number on the call:
  1. **Cash funding** — most useful, most flexible.
  2. **In-kind contributions** — hardware, software licenses/credits,
     event space.
  3. **Mentors/guest speakers** — real but lower-value than the above.
  4. **Just interest/awareness** — the floor, not the goal, but still
     worth keeping the door open on a first, exploratory call.

### Lead-strength heuristic (per VoltPop, 2026-08-02)

A general guide, not a hard-and-fast rule — experiential judgment, not
an empirical formula. Use it to compare leads at a glance, but weigh
each stakeholder on its own facts rather than mechanically ranking by
this alone. Rough priority order: **capability > desire > connection.**

- **Capability** — can this body actually provide grant/donation
  funding? A VSU advisory council counts as its own capable body (the
  council itself can provide a grant), separate from any company its
  members represent.
- **Desire** — is there actual willingness/appetite to fund something
  like this (a stated CSR/giving program, a track record of similar
  gifts), not just plausible interest.
- **Connection** — a pre-existing personal or institutional
  relationship. Strengthens a lead, but doesn't substitute for
  capability or desire — connection alone (e.g. board/council
  membership with no known giving program or funding role) isn't
  enough to justify solicitation.
- **Doubly strong:** a person who represents *two* capable/willing
  bodies at once (e.g. sits on a VSU council **and** works for a
  company with its own giving program) — count both angles rather than
  picking one.
- **Most potent:** a lead where all three — capability, desire, and
  connection — are present at once. That combination outranks any
  single factor alone, including a doubly-strong capability match with
  no real connection. James Beazley (Dominion Energy capability +
  demonstrated desire, per Dominion's "already expressed interest" —
  plus Dr. Nicholson's personal connection) is the clearest current
  example.
