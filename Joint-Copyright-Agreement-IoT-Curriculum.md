## RECITALS

**WHEREAS**, the Parties desire to collaborate in the development, creation, and ongoing enhancement of an Internet of Things (IoT) innovation curriculum incorporating artificial intelligence, machine learning, cybersecurity, and entrepreneurship components (the "Curriculum");

**WHEREAS**, each Party will contribute original copyrightable expression, expertise, and resources to the development of the Curriculum with the intent that their contributions be merged into a unified educational product;

**WHEREAS**, the Parties intend to be joint owners of all copyrights in the Curriculum and desire to establish their respective rights and obligations regarding the ownership, use, licensing, and commercialization thereof;

**WHEREAS**, the Parties recognize that under federal copyright law (17 U.S.C. § 201), without this written agreement, they would become joint owners with equal rights to exploit the work independently;

**NOW, THEREFORE**, in consideration of the mutual covenants contained herein, the Parties agree as follows:

## 1. DEFINITIONS

For purposes of this Agreement, the following terms shall have the meanings set forth below:

**1.1 "Curriculum"** means the comprehensive Internet of Things (IoT) innovation educational program, including without limitation: (a) all course materials, lesson plans, syllabi, and instructional content; (b) presentation materials, visual aids, and multimedia resources; (c) assessment tools, examinations, and evaluation methodologies; (d) software code, applications, and technical documentation; (e) teaching methodologies and pedagogical frameworks; and (f) all updates, revisions, modifications, derivative works, and supplementary materials developed in connection therewith. The term "Curriculum" encompasses both the collective work as a unified educational program and each individual component thereof.

**1.2 "Commercial Use"** means any exploitation, utilization, or deployment of the Curriculum in exchange for monetary consideration, including without limitation: (a) licensing agreements with educational institutions, corporations, government agencies, or other entities; (b) fee-based training programs or workshops; (c) consulting services incorporating the Curriculum; and (d) any other revenue-generating activity involving the Curriculum, regardless of whether such revenue is received directly or indirectly.

**1.3 "Non-Commercial Use"** means utilization of the Curriculum for: (a) internal educational or training purposes within a Party's own organization without external revenue generation; (b) academic research and scholarly activities; (c) professional development of a Party's own personnel; and (d) demonstration or presentation purposes to prospective licensees, provided no licensing fee is charged for such demonstration.

**1.4 "Major Update"** means any modification, revision, or enhancement to the Curriculum that: (a) substantially alters the educational content, learning objectives, or pedagogical approach; (b) adds new subject matter modules or removes existing core components; (c) changes the fundamental structure or delivery methodology; or (d) would reasonably be expected to affect the market value or commercial appeal of the Curriculum.

**1.5 "Licensing Revenue"** means all monetary consideration received in connection with the Commercial Use of the Curriculum, including license fees, royalties, service fees directly attributable to Curriculum delivery, and any other compensation, but excluding reimbursement of reasonable out-of-pocket expenses actually incurred and documented.

**1.6 "Academic Institution"** means any accredited college, university, community college, or other post-secondary educational institution, whether public or private, including their affiliated research centers, extension programs, and continuing education divisions.

## 2. JOINT OWNERSHIP

**2.1 Establishment of Joint Copyright Ownership.** The Parties hereby acknowledge and agree that they are joint owners of the copyright in the Curriculum as a collective work under federal copyright law (17 U.S.C. § 101 et seq.). Each Party holds an equal, undivided one-third (1/3) interest in the copyright, with no Party holding a greater or lesser ownership percentage than any other Party. This joint ownership extends to all existing and future components of the Curriculum developed during the term of this Agreement.

**2.2 Joint Work Under Federal Law.** The Parties expressly acknowledge that: (a) the Curriculum constitutes a "joint work" as defined in 17 U.S.C. § 101, being a work prepared by two or more authors with the intention that their contributions be merged into inseparable or interdependent parts of a unitary whole; (b) each Party has contributed and will continue to contribute original copyrightable expression to the Curriculum with the mutual intention that such contributions be combined into a unified educational program; and (c) absent this Agreement, each Party would possess independent rights to exploit the entire Curriculum under the doctrine of joint ownership, subject only to an obligation to account to co-owners for profits received.

**2.3 Copyright Registration and Protection.** Builder Tech LLC shall serve as the designated agent for copyright registration purposes and shall file appropriate registration applications with the United States Copyright Office listing all three Parties as joint copyright owners. The costs of initial registration and any subsequent copyright-related filings shall be shared equally among the Parties. Each Party agrees to cooperate fully in the registration process, including executing any documents reasonably required by the Copyright Office. In the event Builder Tech LLC is unable or unwilling to serve as registration agent, the Parties shall designate an alternative agent by mutual written consent.

**2.4 Ownership of Individual Contributions.** While maintaining joint ownership of the Curriculum as a collective work, each Party retains ownership of any pre-existing intellectual property, methodologies, or materials that such Party contributes to the Curriculum, subject to the license grants contained herein. No Party shall acquire any ownership interest in another Party's pre-existing intellectual property by virtue of its incorporation into the Curriculum.

**2.5 Third Party Materials.** The Parties acknowledge that the Curriculum may incorporate certain third-party materials used under license, fair use, or other legal authorization. No Party shall acquire ownership rights in such third-party materials, and each Party agrees to comply with all applicable licensing terms and copyright restrictions applicable to such materials.

**2.6 Moral Rights and Attribution.** To the extent permitted by law, each Party waives any moral rights in the Curriculum that might otherwise restrict the joint exploitation thereof, provided that appropriate attribution to all Parties is maintained in accordance with Section 6 of this Agreement. This waiver is intended to facilitate the commercial licensing contemplated herein without prejudicing any Party's right to proper attribution.

## 3. LICENSING CONSENT AND DELIVERY

**3.1 Non-Commercial Use Rights.** Each Party retains the unrestricted right to use the Curriculum for Non-Commercial Use as defined herein, without requiring consent from or providing notice to the other Parties. Such Non-Commercial Use includes, without limitation, internal training, research and development activities, academic presentations, and demonstrations to prospective licensees that do not result in immediate revenue generation.

**3.2 Commercial Licensing Consent Requirement.** No Party may enter into any agreement for Commercial Use of the Curriculum, or any portion thereof, without first obtaining the prior written consent of the other two Parties. Such consent may be granted, conditioned, or withheld in each Party's sole and absolute discretion. The requesting Party must provide the other Parties with reasonable advance notice (not less than fifteen (15) business days) and sufficient detail regarding the proposed licensing arrangement, including: (a) the identity of the proposed licensee; (b) the scope of the proposed license; (c) the proposed licensing fee and payment terms; (d) the duration of the proposed license; and (e) any other material terms of the proposed arrangement.

**3.3 Consent Process and Timeline.** Upon receipt of a licensing proposal pursuant to Section 3.2, each non-requesting Party shall have ten (10) business days to respond in writing with either: (a) consent to the proposal as presented; (b) consent subject to specified conditions; or (c) refusal of consent. Failure to respond within the specified time period shall be deemed consent to the proposal as presented. If conditional consent is given, the Parties shall negotiate in good faith to resolve any conditions within an additional ten (10) business days.

**3.4 Participation Rights and Obligations.** When seeking consent for a Commercial Use opportunity, the requesting Party may, but is not obligated to, invite the other Parties to participate in the delivery of educational services under the license. Similarly, the non-requesting Parties may, but are not obligated to, participate in such delivery. The decision whether to participate in service delivery shall not affect any Party's right to revenue sharing as set forth in Section 4 hereof. All Parties acknowledge that curriculum licensing and service delivery are separate and distinct arrangements.

**3.5 Exclusive Licensing Restrictions.** No Party may grant any exclusive license or exclusive right to use the Curriculum without the unanimous written consent of all Parties. Any purported grant of exclusive rights without such unanimous consent shall be null and void and constitute a material breach of this Agreement.

**3.6 Derivative Works and Modifications.** Each Party may create derivative works based on the Curriculum for the purpose of customizing the Curriculum for specific licensees or market segments, provided that: (a) such derivative works maintain appropriate attribution to all Parties as required by Section 6; (b) the creating Party obtains necessary consents for Commercial Use of such derivative works in accordance with this Section 3; and (c) such derivative works do not materially diminish the commercial value or market appeal of the original Curriculum.

**3.7 Academic Institution Considerations.** In seeking consent for licensing arrangements with Academic Institutions, the Parties acknowledge the importance of: (a) maintaining academic freedom and scholarly integrity; (b) complying with applicable accreditation standards; (c) respecting institutional policies regarding curriculum content and delivery methods; and (d) ensuring compatibility with standard academic calendars and credit structures. Consent decisions should take into account these academic considerations alongside commercial factors.

## 4. MANDATORY REVENUE SHARING

**4.1 Equal Revenue Distribution.** All Licensing Revenue generated from any Commercial Use of the Curriculum shall be distributed equally among the Parties, with each Party receiving exactly one-third (33.33%) of such revenue, regardless of: (a) which Party negotiated, executed, or administered the licensing agreement; (b) whether all Parties participated in the delivery of educational services under the license; (c) the extent or nature of any individual Party's involvement in the specific licensing engagement; (d) the identity or type of licensee; or (e) any other factor. This equal distribution obligation is absolute and may not be waived, modified, or circumvented without the unanimous written consent of all Parties. Notwithstanding the foregoing, any Party may relinquish its own share of Licensing Revenue in accordance with Section 9.3, without the consent of the other Parties.

**4.2 Minimum Licensing Fee Standards.** To protect the commercial value of the Curriculum and ensure fair compensation to all Parties, the licensing fee for any Commercial Use engagement shall be established according to the following principles: (a) the fee must be commercially reasonable when compared to similar educational curriculum products in the technology and innovation training market; (b) the fee must reflect the comprehensive nature and commercial value of the Curriculum; (c) the fee may not be artificially reduced to diminish other Parties' revenue shares; and (d) any fee reduction for strategic, promotional, or competitive purposes requires the prior written consent of all Parties. The Parties shall establish specific minimum fee schedules by separate written agreement within ninety (90) days of executing this Agreement.

**4.3 Revenue Calculation and Expenses.** For purposes of revenue sharing calculations: (a) Licensing Revenue includes all monetary consideration received in connection with Curriculum licensing, including upfront fees, periodic payments, royalties, and success fees; (b) reasonable, documented out-of-pocket expenses directly attributable to a specific licensing engagement may be deducted from gross revenue before calculating shares, provided such expenses are approved in advance by all Parties or fall within pre-approved categories established by mutual agreement; (c) general business expenses, overhead, and indirect costs may not be deducted from Licensing Revenue; and (d) the licensing Party must provide detailed accounting documentation supporting any expense deductions.

**4.4 Payment and Reporting Obligations.** The Party receiving Licensing Revenue (the "Collecting Party") shall: (a) within fifteen (15) days of receipt of any payment from a licensee, provide written notice to the other Parties specifying the amount received and the licensing engagement to which it relates; (b) within forty-five (45) days of receipt of any payment, distribute the appropriate revenue shares to the other Parties via wire transfer or other immediately available funds; (c) within sixty (60) days of completion of each licensing engagement, provide a detailed accounting statement showing all revenue received, any approved expense deductions, and the calculation of distributed amounts; and (d) maintain complete and accurate records of all licensing transactions for a period of not less than seven (7) years and make such records available for inspection by the other Parties upon reasonable notice.

**4.5 Remedies for Non-Payment.** In the event any Collecting Party fails to distribute revenue shares as required herein, the non-receiving Parties shall be entitled to: (a) immediate payment of all overdue amounts plus interest at the rate of one and one-half percent (1.5%) per month from the date payment was due; (b) reimbursement of all reasonable costs and expenses (including attorneys' fees) incurred in collecting overdue amounts; (c) access to all records relating to the relevant licensing transactions; and (d) such other remedies as may be available at law or in equity. Repeated failures to comply with payment obligations shall constitute grounds for termination of the defaulting Party's rights under this Agreement.

**4.6 Tax Considerations.** Each Party shall be responsible for all taxes arising from its receipt of revenue shares under this Agreement. The Collecting Party shall provide appropriate tax documentation (including IRS Forms 1099 or other required forms) to the other Parties as necessary for their tax reporting obligations.

**4.7 Audit Rights.** Each Party shall have the right, upon thirty (30) days' written notice and at its own expense, to audit the books and records of any other Party relating to Curriculum licensing activities. Such audits shall be conducted during normal business hours by qualified independent accountants bound by appropriate confidentiality obligations. If any audit reveals an underpayment exceeding five percent (5%) of the amount that should have been distributed, the audited Party shall bear the cost of the audit in addition to correcting the underpayment.

## 5. CURRICULUM DEVELOPMENT

**5.1 Ongoing Enhancement and Updates.** The Parties acknowledge that the Curriculum is intended to be a living educational product that will require periodic updates, enhancements, and modifications to maintain its relevance, accuracy, and commercial value. The Parties may collectively undertake such updates through mutual agreement and collaborative effort, with each Party contributing expertise in their respective areas of specialization. All updates and enhancements to the Curriculum shall automatically become subject to the joint ownership and revenue sharing provisions of this Agreement.

**5.2 Major Update Approval Process.** Any Major Update to the Curriculum shall require the prior written approval of at least two (2) of the three (3) Parties. The Party proposing a Major Update shall provide the other Parties with: (a) a detailed description of the proposed changes; (b) the educational and commercial rationale for such changes; (c) an estimated timeline for implementation; (d) projected costs and resource requirements; and (e) an assessment of potential impact on existing licensees and ongoing licensing activities. The other Parties shall have twenty (20) business days to review and respond to any Major Update proposal.

**5.3 Minor Updates and Corrections.** Updates that do not constitute Major Updates, including without limitation corrections of factual errors, updates to reflect technological developments, minor pedagogical improvements, and formatting or presentation enhancements, may be implemented by any Party without prior consent, provided that: (a) the updating Party provides advance notice to the other Parties describing the proposed changes; (b) such changes do not materially alter the scope, focus, or commercial positioning of the Curriculum; and (c) appropriate attribution to all Parties is maintained.

**5.4 Individual Contributions and Enhancement.** When any Party contributes new materials, content, or enhancements to the Curriculum, such contributions shall: (a) automatically become subject to the joint ownership provisions of this Agreement; (b) be made available to all Parties for use in connection with Curriculum licensing activities; (c) maintain consistency with the overall educational objectives and quality standards of the Curriculum; and (d) comply with all applicable legal and regulatory requirements. No Party may claim individual ownership of contributions made to the collective Curriculum during the term of this Agreement.

**5.5 Version Control and Documentation.** The Parties shall establish and maintain appropriate version control procedures to ensure: (a) accurate tracking of all changes and updates to the Curriculum; (b) proper attribution of contributions by each Party; (c) maintenance of historical versions for reference and potential reversion; (d) coordination among the Parties to avoid conflicting modifications; and (e) clear documentation of the current authoritative version for licensing purposes. Builder Tech LLC shall serve as the primary coordinator for version control activities, with costs shared equally among all Parties.

**5.6 Quality Standards and Consistency.** All modifications to the Curriculum must meet the following standards: (a) educational content must be accurate, current, and pedagogically sound; (b) materials must be professionally presented and consistent with established formatting and style guidelines; (c) technical content must reflect current industry best practices and standards; (d) all content must be appropriate for the intended audience and learning objectives; and (e) materials must comply with applicable accessibility standards and requirements.

**5.7 Third-Party Integration and Compliance.** When updates involve the integration of third-party materials, software, or content, the updating Party must: (a) ensure proper licensing or authorization for such third-party materials; (b) verify compatibility with existing Curriculum components; (c) assess potential impact on current licensing arrangements; (d) obtain necessary consents from other Parties if such integration affects their rights or obligations; and (e) maintain documentation of all third-party licenses and authorizations.

**5.8 Academic Standards Alignment.** In developing and updating the Curriculum, the Parties shall endeavor to maintain alignment with: (a) relevant accreditation standards for higher education institutions; (b) industry certification requirements and professional development standards; (c) emerging best practices in technology education and workforce development; (d) federal and state educational policy initiatives; and (e) accessibility and inclusion standards applicable to educational materials. Such alignment efforts support the Curriculum's marketability to Academic Institutions and enhance its value proposition.

## 6. ATTRIBUTION AND BRANDING

**6.1 Mandatory Joint Attribution.** All uses, distributions, presentations, and licensing of the Curriculum, or any portion thereof, shall include prominent and appropriate attribution to all three Parties as joint copyright owners and developers. Such attribution shall appear in a conspicuous location and shall state substantially as follows: "This curriculum was jointly developed by Builder Tech LLC, VoltPop LLC, and Explay LLC. All rights reserved." The specific format and placement of attribution may be adapted to suit different media and presentation contexts while maintaining the substance and prominence of the required acknowledgment.

**6.2 Copyright Notices and Legal Markings.** All Curriculum materials shall bear appropriate copyright notices in the following format: "© [Year] Builder Tech LLC, VoltPop LLC, and Explay LLC. All rights reserved." Additional legal markings, such as trademark notices, patent citations, or third-party attributions, shall be included as appropriate and required by law. These notices shall be maintained in all versions, updates, and derivative works of the Curriculum.

**6.3 Individual Company Branding Rights.** Each Party retains the right to use its own corporate branding, logos, trademarks, and marketing materials when delivering educational services that incorporate the Curriculum, provided that: (a) such individual branding does not obscure or diminish the required joint attribution; (b) the joint copyright ownership is clearly acknowledged in all marketing and promotional materials; (c) no Party may represent itself as the sole creator or owner of the Curriculum; and (d) individual branding does not create confusion regarding the collaborative nature of the Curriculum's development.

**6.4 Marketing and Promotional Materials.** When creating marketing materials, proposals, or promotional content that references the Curriculum, each Party shall: (a) include appropriate acknowledgment of the collaborative development and joint ownership; (b) represent the Curriculum accurately and consistently with agreed-upon descriptions and positioning; (c) avoid any claims or implications of exclusive ownership or control; (d) provide copies of significant marketing materials to the other Parties for informational purposes; and (e) ensure that all representations regarding the Curriculum's content, scope, and benefits are accurate and substantiated.

**6.5 Academic and Professional Recognition.** In academic presentations, research publications, professional development contexts, and similar scholarly or professional communications, the Parties shall ensure proper attribution through: (a) citation in bibliographies and reference lists when the Curriculum is discussed or referenced; (b) acknowledgment in presentations that incorporate Curriculum content; (c) recognition in professional biographies or credentials statements that mention involvement in Curriculum development; and (d) appropriate attribution in any derivative academic or professional works based on the Curriculum.

**6.6 Third-Party Attribution Requirements.** When licensing the Curriculum to third parties, the licensing Party shall ensure that licensees understand and comply with attribution requirements by: (a) including specific attribution obligations in all licensing agreements; (b) providing licensees with approved attribution language and formatting guidelines; (c) monitoring licensee compliance with attribution requirements; (d) taking appropriate action to correct any attribution failures or omissions; and (e) reporting any significant attribution issues to the other Parties.

**6.7 Digital and Online Attribution.** For digital presentations, online courses, websites, and electronic materials incorporating the Curriculum, attribution shall be provided through: (a) prominent display on title pages, course introduction screens, or website headers; (b) inclusion in metadata, course catalogs, and search engine descriptions; (c) links to Party websites or professional profiles where technically feasible and appropriate; and (d) maintenance of attribution in downloadable materials, printable versions, and archived content.

**6.8 Press and Media Relations.** When engaging with press, media, or public relations activities that reference the Curriculum, each Party shall: (a) accurately represent the collaborative nature of the Curriculum's development; (b) provide advance notice to other Parties of significant media opportunities or press releases; (c) offer opportunities for joint participation in major media events or press activities; (d) avoid exclusive claims or misleading representations about individual contributions; and (e) coordinate messaging to ensure consistency and accuracy in public communications.

## 7. CONFIDENTIALITY

**7.1 Definition of Confidential Information.** "Confidential Information" means any non-public business, financial, technical, or strategic information that one Party (the "Disclosing Party") discloses to another Party (the "Receiving Party") in connection with this Agreement, whether orally, in writing, electronically, or by demonstration, that is either marked or identified as confidential or would reasonably be understood to be confidential given the nature of the information and the circumstances of its disclosure. **7.2 Status of the Curriculum.** The released Curriculum is not Confidential Information. The Parties intend for the Curriculum to be taught, demonstrated, licensed, and shared with students, instructors, and licensees, and nothing in this Section 7 limits any Party's rights under Sections 3 and 6 to use, deliver, or disclose the Curriculum for those purposes. The Parties' ownership of and control over the Curriculum are protected by federal copyright law and by Sections 2 through 6 of this Agreement, not by this Section 7.

**7.3 Obligations of the Receiving Party.** The Receiving Party shall: (a) use Confidential Information solely to perform its obligations and exercise its rights under this Agreement; (b) protect Confidential Information using at least the same degree of care it uses to protect its own confidential information of similar importance, and in no event less than reasonable care; and (c) not disclose Confidential Information to any person except as permitted by Sections 7.4 and 7.6.

**7.4 Permitted Disclosures.** The Receiving Party may disclose Confidential Information to its members, managers, employees, contractors, and professional advisors (including attorneys, accountants, and tax advisors) who need to know such information for purposes of this Agreement and who are bound, by written agreement or professional duty, to confidentiality obligations no less protective than those in this Section 7. With respect to an arrangement approved under Section 3, the Receiving Party may also disclose to the prospective or actual licensee, Academic Institution, or joint venture partner only those terms necessary to negotiate or perform that arrangement, and only under written confidentiality terms no less protective than those in this Section 7. The Receiving Party shall be responsible for any breach of this Section 7 by any person to whom it discloses Confidential Information.

**7.5 Exclusions.** Confidential Information does not include information that the Receiving Party can demonstrate: (a) is or becomes publicly available through no fault of the Receiving Party; (b) was lawfully known to the Receiving Party before disclosure, without an obligation of confidentiality; (c) is lawfully received from a third party without restriction on disclosure; or (d) is independently developed by the Receiving Party without use of or reference to the Disclosing Party's Confidential Information.

**7.6 Legally Required Disclosure and Public Records.** The Receiving Party may disclose Confidential Information to the extent required by law, regulation, subpoena, court order, or other legal process, provided that, where legally permitted, the Receiving Party: (a) gives the Disclosing Party prompt written notice; (b) reasonably cooperates with any effort by the Disclosing Party to obtain a protective order or other remedy; and (c) discloses only the portion of the Confidential Information that is legally required. Code § 2.2-3700 et seq.) or other applicable public records laws. A Party submitting another Party's Confidential Information to a public body shall, before submission, use reasonable efforts to invoke any available protection for trade secrets or proprietary information in the manner required by applicable law, including, where applicable, Va. Code § 2.2-4342(F), and shall notify the other Parties of such submission. Nothing in this Agreement shall be construed to restrict any public body's compliance with public records laws.

**7.7 Student Information.** Student education records and personally identifiable information about students are not governed by this Section 7 and are not to be exchanged among the Parties under this Agreement. Any access by a Party to student information in connection with delivering the Curriculum shall be governed by the applicable agreement with the Academic Institution or other licensee and by applicable law, including the Family Educational Rights and Privacy Act (20 U.S.C. § 1232g; 34 C.F.R. Part 99). No Party shall disclose student information to another Party except as such agreement and applicable law expressly permit.

**7.8 Duration.** The obligations in this Section 7 shall apply during the term of this Agreement and for three (3) years following its termination; provided, however, that with respect to any Confidential Information that constitutes a trade secret under the Virginia Uniform Trade Secrets Act (Va. Code § 59.1-336 et seq.) or the Defend Trade Secrets Act (18 U.S.C. § 1836 et seq.), such obligations shall continue for as long as the information remains a trade secret.

**7.9 Return or Destruction.** Upon termination of this Agreement, or upon the Disclosing Party's written request once the Receiving Party no longer requires the information for purposes of this Agreement, the Receiving Party shall return or destroy the Disclosing Party's Confidential Information and confirm in writing that it has done so; provided that the Receiving Party may retain: (a) copies required by law or by its record-keeping obligations under Section 4.4(d); and (b) copies stored in routine electronic backups that are not readily accessible. Any retained copies shall remain subject to this Section 7.

**7.10 Remedies.** The Parties acknowledge that unauthorized use or disclosure of Confidential Information may cause irreparable harm for which monetary damages would be an inadequate remedy. Notwithstanding Section 10, the Disclosing Party may seek temporary or preliminary injunctive relief from a court of competent jurisdiction to prevent any actual or threatened breach of this Section 7, in addition to any other remedy available at law or in equity.

Nothing in this Agreement prohibits any person from reporting a possible violation of law to a government agency.

**7.12 Relationship to Other Agreements.** This Section 7 governs only information exchanged among the Parties under this Agreement. Confidentiality obligations under any licensing agreement, joint venture agreement, or agreement with an Academic Institution shall be set forth in that agreement and shall govern information received under it. Where this Section 7 and such other agreement both apply to the same information, each Party shall comply with the more protective obligation; provided that nothing in this Section 7 shall prevent any Party from performing its obligations under such other agreement.

## 9. TERM AND TERMINATION

**9.1 Term.** This Agreement shall commence on the Agreement Date and shall continue for the full duration of the copyright in the Curriculum, unless earlier terminated by the mutual written consent of all Parties or as otherwise provided in this Section 9.

**9.2 Withdrawal from Development.** A Party may withdraw from ongoing Curriculum development activities upon ninety (90) days' prior written notice to the other Parties. Upon the effective date of withdrawal: (a) the withdrawing Party shall retain its full ownership interest in the Curriculum and its right to Licensing Revenue under Section 4; (b) the withdrawing Party shall be released from any obligation to contribute to future development under Section 5 and from its share of development costs incurred after such date; and (c) the withdrawing Party shall continue to be bound by, and entitled to the benefit of, all other provisions of this Agreement, including the consent rights under Section 3. A Party that wishes to give up its ownership interest, its right to Licensing Revenue, or both shall do so under Section 9.3.

**9.3 Voluntary Relinquishment.** Any Party (the "Relinquishing Party") may, at any time and in its sole discretion, voluntarily relinquish: (A) its right to Licensing Revenue while retaining its ownership interest; (B) its ownership interest while retaining its right to Licensing Revenue; or (C) both its ownership interest and its right to Licensing Revenue, in each case as provided in this Section 9.3. Each Party acknowledges that any relinquishment may have tax consequences and shall consult its own tax advisor before relinquishing; no Party makes any representation to another regarding such consequences.

**(A) Relinquishment of Compensation Only.** A Party may relinquish its right to receive Licensing Revenue while retaining its full ownership interest in the Curriculum, subject to the following: (i) the relinquishment shall be made by a written instrument signed by an authorized representative of the Relinquishing Party and delivered to each other Party before the Licensing Revenue it covers is received by any Collecting Party; (ii) the instrument shall state whether it applies to a specified licensing engagement or to all Licensing Revenue received after its effective date; (iii) the relinquishment shall be irrevocable as to the Licensing Revenue it covers; (iv) the relinquished share shall be distributed equally among the other Parties that hold an ownership interest and have not relinquished their right to Licensing Revenue, and shall not reduce the licensing fee charged to any licensee; and (v) the Relinquishing Party shall retain all other rights under this Agreement, including its ownership interest, its consent and approval rights under Sections 3 and 5, attribution under Section 6, and audit rights under Section 4.7.

**(B) Relinquishment of Ownership Only.** A Party may relinquish its entire ownership interest in the Curriculum while retaining a right to receive Licensing Revenue (a "Retained Revenue Interest"), subject to Section 9.3(D) and the following: (i) the Retained Revenue Interest shall be fixed at one-third (1/3) of all Licensing Revenue, the same share the Relinquishing Party would have received had it not relinquished its ownership interest, and shall not be increased, reduced, or renegotiated by reason of the relinquishment or of any Party's level of contribution; (ii) the Retained Revenue Interest shall apply to all Licensing Revenue received on or after the effective date, including revenue from updates and enhancements to the Curriculum, and shall continue for so long as Licensing Revenue is payable under this Agreement; (iii) the Relinquishing Party shall continue to receive the notices, distributions, and accounting statements required by Section 4.4 and shall retain its audit rights under Section 4.7; (iv) the Relinquishing Party shall have no consent or approval rights under Sections 3 or 5 and no right to use, license, or deliver the Curriculum except as provided in Section 9.3(D)(e); and (v) the Relinquishing Party may later relinquish its Retained Revenue Interest, in whole, by a signed written instrument delivered to each other Party, whereupon Section 9.3(C) shall apply as of the effective date of that instrument.

**(C) Relinquishment of Ownership and Compensation.** A Party may relinquish its entire ownership interest in the Curriculum together with all rights to Licensing Revenue, subject to Section 9.3(D). The Relinquishing Party's one-third (1/3) share of Licensing Revenue received on or after the effective date shall be distributed in accordance with Section 9.3(D)(j).

**(D) Terms Applicable to Any Relinquishment of Ownership.** The following terms apply to any relinquishment under Section 9.3(B) or 9.3(C):
(a) *Written Instrument.* Relinquishment shall be effective only by a written instrument of assignment signed by an authorized representative of the Relinquishing Party, as required by 17 U.S.C. § 204(a), and delivered to each other Party. The instrument shall state whether the relinquishment is made under Section 9.3(B) or 9.3(C) and its effective date, which shall not be earlier than the date of delivery.
(b) *Entire Ownership Interest Only.* Relinquishment shall apply to the Relinquishing Party's entire ownership interest. Partial relinquishment of an ownership interest is not permitted.
(c) *Assignment to Remaining Owners.* The Relinquishing Party's ownership interest shall be assigned in equal shares to the Parties that continue to hold an ownership interest (the "Remaining Owners"), who shall thereafter hold equal, undivided interests in the Curriculum. Each Party hereby consents in advance to accept any such assignment. No Party may assign its ownership interest to any person other than the Remaining Owners except as provided in Section 9.5.
(d) *Irrevocability and Prospective Effect.* Relinquishment shall be irrevocable upon delivery of the signed instrument and shall not affect the Relinquishing Party's right to its share of Licensing Revenue received by any Collecting Party before the effective date, which shall be distributed in accordance with Section 4.
(e) *Post-Relinquishment Use.* After the effective date, the Relinquishing Party shall have no right to use, license, or deliver the Curriculum except under a license granted in accordance with Section 3; provided that the Relinquishing Party may accurately describe its role in developing the Curriculum and may display limited excerpts of its own contributions for portfolio and professional reference purposes.
(f) *Existing Licenses.* Relinquishment shall not affect the validity of any license or other agreement for Commercial Use entered into before the effective date, and the Remaining Owners shall succeed to all rights of the Relinquishing Party thereunder, subject to any Retained Revenue Interest.
(g) *Attribution.* The Remaining Owners shall continue to credit the Relinquishing Party as a developer (but not as an owner) of those versions of the Curriculum to which it contributed, unless the Relinquishing Party waives such credit in writing. The copyright notice required by Section 6.2 shall be updated to list only the Remaining Owners.
(h) *Continuing and Released Obligations.* The Relinquishing Party shall remain bound by Section 7 and by Section 8 with respect to its contributions, and shall be released from all obligations arising after the effective date that apply to owners, including cost-sharing obligations under Sections 2.3 and 5.5.
(i) *Recordation and Further Assurances.* The Remaining Owners may record the instrument of assignment with the United States Copyright Office under 17 U.S.C. § 205. The Relinquishing Party shall execute such further documents as are reasonably necessary to give effect to the relinquishment.
(j) *Governance and Revenue After Relinquishment.* Where two Remaining Owners remain, any provision of this Agreement requiring the approval or consent of two (2) of the three (3) Parties shall require the approval or consent of both Remaining Owners. Licensing Revenue shall continue to be calculated in one-third (1/3) shares: each Party that retains its right to Licensing Revenue, whether as an owner or through a Retained Revenue Interest, shall receive one-third (1/3), and any one-third (1/3) share that has been relinquished shall be divided equally among the Remaining Owners that have not relinquished their right to Licensing Revenue. A Retained Revenue Interest shall never exceed one-third (1/3). Where only one Remaining Owner remains, that Party shall hold sole ownership of the Curriculum, and this Agreement shall terminate, subject to Section 9.8, except that it shall continue in effect as necessary to give effect to any Retained Revenue Interest.

**9.4 Material Breach.** If a Party materially breaches this Agreement, either of the other Parties may deliver written notice describing the breach in reasonable detail. If the breach is not cured within thirty (30) days after such notice (or ten (10) business days in the case of a failure to distribute revenue shares under Section 4.4), the non-breaching Parties, acting jointly, may: (a) suspend the breaching Party's consent and approval rights under Sections 3 and 5 until the breach is cured; (b) offset any amounts owed by the breaching Party against revenue shares otherwise payable to it; and (c) pursue any remedy available under Section 10. ## 10. DISPUTE RESOLUTION

**10.1 Informal Resolution.** In the event of any dispute, controversy, or claim among the Parties arising out of or relating to this Agreement or the Curriculum, including its breach, termination, interpretation, or validity (a "Dispute"), any Party may deliver written notice of the Dispute to the other Parties describing its nature in reasonable detail. Within fifteen (15) business days after delivery of such notice, authorized representatives of the Parties involved, each with authority to resolve the Dispute, shall meet in person or by video conference and negotiate in good faith to resolve it.

**10.2 Mediation.** If the Dispute is not resolved within thirty (30) days after delivery of the notice under Section 10.1, any Party involved may submit the Dispute to non-binding mediation before a single mediator mutually selected by the Parties involved or, if they cannot agree within ten (10) business days, appointed by the American Arbitration Association under its Commercial Mediation Procedures. The mediation shall take place in the Commonwealth of Virginia or by video conference, and the Parties involved shall share the mediator's fees and administrative costs equally. Communications made in the course of mediation shall be confidential to the extent provided by Va. Code § 8.01-576.10 and other applicable law.

**10.3 Binding Arbitration.** Any Dispute not resolved within sixty (60) days after the commencement of mediation, or such longer period as the Parties involved agree in writing, shall be resolved exclusively by final and binding arbitration administered by the American Arbitration Association under its Commercial Arbitration Rules then in effect. This agreement to arbitrate shall be governed by the Federal Arbitration Act (9 U.S.C. § 1 et seq.) and, to the extent not inconsistent therewith, the Virginia Uniform Arbitration Act (Va. Code § 8.01-581.01 et seq.).

**10.4 Arbitration Procedures.** (a) The arbitration shall be conducted before a single arbitrator who is an attorney with experience in intellectual property or commercial licensing matters. (b) The seat of arbitration shall be the Commonwealth of Virginia, and hearings may be conducted in person in Virginia or by video conference as the arbitrator directs. (c) The arbitrator shall apply the governing law set forth in Section 10.6, shall have no authority to award damages excluded by, or to modify, the terms of this Agreement, and shall issue a reasoned written award. (d) The arbitration and all related filings, testimony, and awards shall be confidential, except as necessary to enforce the award or as required by law. (e) The Parties involved shall share the administrative fees and the arbitrator's compensation equally, and each shall bear its own attorneys' fees and costs, except as otherwise provided in Sections 4.5 and 8.8 or where the arbitrator finds that a claim or defense was asserted in bad faith. (f) Judgment on the award may be entered in any court of competent jurisdiction.

**10.5 Exceptions.** Notwithstanding Sections 10.1 through 10.4: (a) any Party may seek temporary or preliminary injunctive relief as provided in Section 7.10, or to prevent imminent unauthorized use or licensing of the Curriculum in violation of Section 3, without first completing the procedures in this Section 10; (b) any Party may bring a claim within the jurisdictional limit of a small claims court in the Commonwealth of Virginia in that court; and (c) any Party may bring an action to compel arbitration or to confirm, modify, or vacate an arbitration award. Seeking such relief shall not waive any Party's right to arbitrate.

**10.6 Governing Law.** This Agreement shall be governed by and construed in accordance with the laws of the Commonwealth of Virginia, without regard to its conflict of laws principles, except that matters concerning the existence, ownership, scope, validity, and infringement of copyrights in the Curriculum shall be governed by the Copyright Act of 1976, as amended (17 U.S.C. § 101 et seq.), and other applicable federal law.

**10.7 Venue and Jurisdiction.** Subject to the obligation to arbitrate under this Section 10, any action permitted to be brought in court shall be brought exclusively in the state courts located in the Commonwealth of Virginia or the United States District Court for the Eastern District of Virginia, and each Party consents to the personal jurisdiction of and venue in such courts.

**10.8 Continued Performance.** During the pendency of any Dispute, each Party shall continue to perform its obligations under this Agreement, including distribution of undisputed Licensing Revenue under Section 4 and performance of its obligations to licensees, and no Party shall suspend, interrupt, or interfere with any license or delivery of the Curriculum to a licensee by reason of the Dispute.

**10.9 Licensees and Third Parties.** This Section 10 applies only to Disputes among the Parties. Nothing in this Agreement requires any licensee, Academic Institution, public body, joint venture partner, or other third party to participate in the procedures set forth in this Section 10, and disputes with any such person shall be governed by the terms of the applicable agreement with that person.

**10.10 Tolling.** All applicable statutes of limitations and time-based defenses shall be tolled from the date a notice of Dispute is delivered under Section 10.1 until the earlier of the conclusion of mediation under Section 10.2 or ninety (90) days after such notice, and the Parties shall take any action reasonably required to give effect to such tolling.
